MASIS: Multi-Agent Supplier Intelligence System

Explain it:
Multi-Agent OrchestrationLangGraphHybrid 3-Tier RoutingKnowledge GraphsBounded Verification

The problem

A procurement team's knowledge is scattered: contracts in one system, spend in another, supplier relationships in a third. Answering one real question, such as "who are we single-sourced with and out of contract?", means logging into several tools and stitching the answers together by hand. MASIS lets you ask in plain English and does the stitching for you, and it can show its work.

The approach

MASIS orchestrates six databases across three modalities (vector, SQL, graph) through a five-stage agentic pipeline. A hybrid 3-tier router (LLM reasoning, then embedding similarity, then domain heuristics) decides which sources a question needs, and bounded verification loops gate the answer on correctness and provenance before synthesis. It serves both human users and other AI agents, and consolidates prior in-house research (spend categorization, text-to-SQL, RFx retrieval) into one orchestrated layer, benchmarked across five enterprise retrieval architectures on 80 curated queries.

Why it matters

  • One natural-language interface over vector, relational and graph sources, with no tool-switching or manual reconciliation.
  • Bounded verification gates answers on correctness and provenance, which is essential for high-stakes procurement decisions.
  • Serves both people and other agents, turning siloed prior work into one reusable intelligence layer.

MARLIN: Deep RL for inland freight pricing

Explain it:
Deep RLConstrained MDPsPPOA2CSACDQNTD3Dynamic PricingEnsemble ML

The problem

Barges are a cheap, low-carbon way to move freight, but only if they run full and on time. Price too high and containers go to trucks; price too low and you leave money on the table and still have to cover fixed costs. The right price changes week to week with demand, weather and how much capacity is already committed. MARLIN learns a pricing policy that adapts to those conditions automatically, instead of relying on a fixed markup that is wrong most of the time.

The approach

Pricing and routing are formulated as a finite-horizon constrained Markov decision process. The state captures committed load, remaining capacity, service-time slack and demand signals; the action sets a price (and allocation) each week; the reward is discounted profit subject to capacity, dwell and service-time constraints. I implement and benchmark value-based and actor-critic algorithms (PPO, A2C, SAC, DQN and TD3) against an operationally grounded, sponsor-calibrated cost model and a transparent shadow-price (cost-plus) baseline.

What the learned policy does

  • In soft demand, it prices below cost-plus to win volume and fill scarce capacity that would otherwise sail empty.
  • In strong demand, it holds price above cost-plus, capturing willingness to pay instead of underselling committed capacity.
  • It respects the shared capacity constraint. Tightening fixed hours reshapes the policy, and some algorithms degrade more gracefully than others under stress.

Q-PROTECT: mapping quantum hardware supply chains

Explain it:
Knowledge GraphsTrade-Flow AnalysisPatent / CPC LinkageScenario Stress-TestingLocal LLMs

The problem

Quantum computers are built from exotic materials, some of which come from just one or two places in the world. If any of those inputs is disrupted, whole families of quantum hardware could stall. Today almost nobody has a clear, company-by-company picture of who depends on what. Q-PROTECT builds that picture so the fragile points are visible in advance, across superconducting, ion-trap and photonic ecosystems.

The approach

I map supply networks in emerging quantum computing using self-hosted search, local language models and provenance-aware property graphs. The graph is auditable: every edge carries provenance, so chokepoints and single-source dependencies can be traced back to the evidence that established them.

Four pillars

  • Firm-network expansion: grow the entity set from a seed of about 160 to 500+ companies across the ecosystem.
  • Patent and CPC linkage: connect materials and components to the qubit architectures that rely on them.
  • Trade-flow analysis: transaction-level import and export evidence to see where inputs actually move.
  • Scenario stress-testing: remove or constrain critical inputs and trace the cascade through the network.
