About

MIT Center for Transportation & Logistics — Deep Knowledge Lab

I'm a PhD candidate at MIT's Center for Transportation & Logistics, where my research studies how AI systems can transform supply chain decision-making under uncertainty.

My research examines how AI and operations research can improve supply chain decisions under uncertainty across three linked areas. First, I build agentic procurement-intelligence systems that combine hybrid retrieval, knowledge graphs, and bounded verification across heterogeneous enterprise data — including a multinational supplier-intelligence deployment and a hyperscaler collaboration on agentic bill-of-materials knowledge graphs. Second, I apply reinforcement learning, dynamic programming, and ensemble learning to freight pricing, routing, market sizing, and decarbonization. Third, I map the supply networks behind emerging technologies — especially quantum computing — using self-hosted search, local language models, and provenance-aware property graphs to surface chokepoints and single-source dependencies.

Before MIT, I held leadership and analytics roles at Amazon (Tokyo), Flock Freight, Zillow, and Arrive Logistics, where I developed pricing strategies, implemented ML systems, and led operations teams. My time as a site leader at Amazon Japan gave me a deep appreciation for cross-cultural leadership and operational excellence at scale.

Featured · Agentic Procurement Intelligence

MASIS — Multi-Agent Supplier Intelligence System

Sponsored research with a global healthcare conglomerate

A 5-stage agentic pipeline — Plan, Route, Retrieve, Verify, Synthesize — that orchestrates six databases across three modalities (vector, SQL, graph) under a single natural-language interface. Hybrid 3-tier routing (LLM reasoning → embedding similarity → domain heuristics) with bounded verification loops enables cross-source procurement queries that previously required three separate tools and manual reconciliation.

The system serves both human users and other AI agents, turning years of prior in-house research (spend categorization, text-to-SQL, RFx RAG) into one orchestrated intelligence layer — benchmarked across five enterprise retrieval architectures on exact-match accuracy, F1, multi-source recall, abstention, latency, and cache performance.

Featured · Reinforcement Learning

MARLIN — Maritime Adaptive Reinforcement Learning for Inland Networks

Constrained-MDP pricing & routing for Mississippi container barge service

Constrained Markov decision processes and stochastic simulators for containerized barge pricing and routing — implementing and benchmarking value-based and actor-critic methods (PPO, A2C, SAC, DQN, TD3) under capacity, service-time, and cost constraints. Paired with ensemble market-sizing across 12,587 freight lanes (XGBoost, CatBoost, SHAP) quantifying serviceable demand under 2030, 2035, and 2040 scenarios.

Featured · Quantum Supply Chains

Q-PROTECT — Quantum Platform Risk Observation through Embedded Company Tracking

MIT CTL — firm-level supply-network research

The first firm-level, multi-platform map of quantum hardware supply chain dependencies across superconducting, ion-trap, and photonic qubit ecosystems. A four-pillar methodology combines firm-network expansion (~160 → 500+ entities), patent and CPC-code linkage of materials to qubit architectures, transaction-level trade-flow analysis (Panjiva, ManifestDB, USITC), and scenario-based stress testing on critical inputs.

The work surfaces single-point failure nodes for materials like helium-3, niobium, nonlinear crystals, and rare earths — building the visibility policymakers need before the next disruption, not after.
