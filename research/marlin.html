MARLIN

Maritime Adaptive Reinforcement Learning for Inland Networks

Deep reinforcement learning for dynamic pricing and routing of containerized barge service on the Mississippi River — learning when to discount to fill capacity and when to hold price to capture value, under real capacity, service-time, and cost constraints.

Deep RL Constrained MDPs PPO · A2C · SAC · DQN · TD3 Dynamic Pricing Ensemble ML
Explain it:

The problem

Barges are a cheap, low-carbon way to move freight — but only if they run full and on time. Price too high and containers go to trucks; price too low and you leave money on the table and still have to cover fixed costs. The right price changes week to week with demand, weather, and how much capacity is already committed.

MARLIN learns a pricing policy that adapts to those conditions automatically, instead of relying on a fixed markup that is wrong most of the time.

Pricing and routing are formulated as a finite-horizon constrained Markov decision process. The state captures committed load, remaining capacity, service-time slack, and demand signals; the action sets a price (and allocation) each week; the reward is discounted profit subject to capacity, dwell, and service-time constraints.

I implement and benchmark value-based and actor-critic algorithms — PPO, A2C, SAC, DQN, and TD3 — against an operationally grounded, sponsor-calibrated cost model and a transparent shadow-price (cost-plus) baseline.

What the learned policy does

  • In soft demand, it prices below cost-plus to win volume and fill scarce capacity that would otherwise sail empty.
  • In strong demand, it holds price above cost-plus, capturing willingness-to-pay instead of underselling committed capacity.
  • It respects the shared capacity constraint — tightening fixed hours reshapes the policy, and some algorithms degrade more gracefully than others under stress.
