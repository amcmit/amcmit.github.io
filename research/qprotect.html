Featured Research · Quantum Supply Chains

Q-PROTECT

Quantum Platform Risk Observation through Embedded Company Tracking

A firm-level, multi-platform map of quantum hardware supply chain dependencies — across superconducting, ion-trap, and photonic qubit ecosystems — built to surface chokepoints and single-source dependencies before a disruption, not after.

Knowledge Graphs Trade-Flow Analysis Patent / CPC Linkage Scenario Stress-Testing Local LLMs
Explain it:

The problem

Quantum computers are built from exotic materials — some of which come from just one or two places in the world. If any of those inputs is disrupted, whole families of quantum hardware could stall. Today almost nobody has a clear, company-by-company picture of who depends on what.

Q-PROTECT builds that picture, so the fragile points are visible in advance.

I map supply networks in emerging quantum computing using self-hosted search, local language models, and provenance-aware property graphs. A four-pillar methodology combines firm-network expansion (~160 → 500+ entities), patent and CPC-code linkage of materials to qubit architectures, transaction-level trade-flow analysis, and scenario-based stress testing on critical inputs.

The graph is auditable — every edge carries provenance — so chokepoints and single-source dependencies can be traced back to the evidence that established them.

Four pillars

  • Firm-network expansion — grow the entity set from a seed of ~160 to 500+ companies across the ecosystem.
  • Patent & CPC linkage — connect materials and components to the qubit architectures that rely on them.
  • Trade-flow analysis — transaction-level import/export evidence to see where inputs actually move.
  • Scenario stress-testing — remove or constrain critical inputs and trace the cascade through the network.
